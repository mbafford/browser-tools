Method. Each episode assumes retirement on January 1 of the start year with your chosen starting balance (default $1,000,000), held for the chosen horizon. The balance compounds by real, inflation-adjusted monthly total returns: price plus reinvested income, in constant dollars. Stock returns use annual Shiller/Damodaran real S&P 500 figures, shaped to place each crash in its actual month (Oct 1929, Oct 1987, Feb and Mar 2020, Sep and Oct 2008, and so on). The stocks/bonds slider blends in real long-term U.S. Treasury total returns for the same years. The two sleeves grow independently, and the rebalance control sets how often they reset to your target mix. It defaults to Never, so a crash permanently shifts the mix toward whatever survived. That is why bonds rescue the 1929 and 2008 windows, where deflation and flight-to-quality helped them, but fail in the 1970s, when high inflation gave bonds negative real returns too.

The horizon slider extends the hold from 10 to 30 years. Each episode uses as much real history as exists, through the end of 2024, so a 1929, 1973, or 1987 retirement can run its full 30 years on actual returns. Only genuinely future months, mainly in the recent 2007, 2020, and 2000-start windows past 2024, fall back to long-run average real returns (about 6.7%/yr for stocks, 2%/yr for bonds), shown as the shaded "projected" region. That part is an assumption, not a forecast.

A withdrawal comes out on the 1st of every month, before that month's return. In "% of live balance" mode each withdrawal is 1/12 of the annual rate applied to the current balance, so your monthly check rises in good times and shrinks in bad ones. In "Fixed $" mode the dollar amount is locked at the opening balance (for example, 3% of $1M is $2,500/mo for the whole horizon) no matter what the portfolio does.

Illustration only. This is an all-equity buy-and-hold by default, chosen to isolate crash and sequence-of-returns behavior; real portfolios diversify. Monthly figures are modeled approximations, faithful to each period's shape but not tick-exact. This is not financial advice, so consult a licensed advisor.
